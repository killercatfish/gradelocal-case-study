GradeLocal — Product Case Study

Joshua Gold · Online math & CS teacher

GradeLocal is an AI feedback assistant I designed and built, demonstrated here with synthetic student data. It drafts personalized feedback emails and retake review sheets from student work. Two design requirements shaped it: every model runs on hardware the teacher owns, so student data never reaches a cloud AI, and the teacher approves every word before a student sees it.

The problem

Specific, encouraging written feedback helps students most, and it takes teachers the most time. Cloud AI tools would make it faster, but sending student work to a third-party server conflicts with student-data privacy policy. Districts have the same concern: they want the time savings, and they also need a clear answer about where student data goes. Designed for: the teacher, who reviews and approves; students, who read the feedback; and parents, who may read it with them.

Key decisions: trading automation for trust

DecisionTradeoff acceptedWhy
Every model runs on local hardware; no cloud API sees student workSmaller, slower modelsIt answers the privacy question completely, not with a policy promise
The teacher approves every email and sheet before a student sees itIt's never "fully automatic"Approval is both the compliance mechanism and the quality bar
Math is checked symbolically; a failed step is dropped, never "repaired"Some questions get no worked solutionA wrong worked solution does more harm than an empty box
When the model can't verify an answer, it's marked "unverified" or left for the teacherMore manual work on hard casesThe tool is honest about what it doesn't know

Problems found and fixed

ProblemFix
A retake score alone doesn't show what was missedBuilt review sheets: quiz screenshots in, a "how to solve it" sheet out, which the teacher edits and approves
The model confidently "solved" a graph question it had misreadQuestions with a figure go to the teacher; the model's own guesses are labeled "unverified"
One quiz layout made the questions unreadableThe reader uses row colors; the quiz's own answer line cross-checks but never sets the answer
Explaining a "mistake" on a question left blank doesn't helpBlank questions are taught from scratch instead
Model-written math showed up as raw LaTeX in emailsMath is written as plain text

Testing

  • 143 automated tests pass on the review-sheet pipeline: reading marks, checking the math, writing steps, rendering, and the server routes.
  • The one-minute demo uses only synthetic data.

How I work as a PM

  • I start from the constraint. Privacy shaped the product before any features did.
  • I treat trust as a feature. The tool says "unverified" rather than guess.
  • I design for what users feel: the app logs time to approve and whether a draft was edited, not model scores.
  • I write decisions down, with dated records of what was chosen, what was rejected, and why.
